# Dragon Bestiary — Part I

Introduction While the Dragon Codex establishes the universal laws that govern dragonkind, the Dragon Bestiary serves a different purpose.

It is a living record of the individual species known to merchant guilds throughout the continent.

The entries contained within are compiled from decades—sometimes centuries—of observations by guildmasters, dragon keepers, explorers, veterinarians, researchers, and merchants who dedicated their lives to understanding these remarkable creatures.

No entry should ever be considered complete.

As guilds continue exploring new lands and working alongside dragons, new discoveries will undoubtedly reshape our understanding of these species.

**How to Read This Bestiary** — Every species follows the same structure.

Some sections describe biological facts.

Others describe merchant practices.

The "Species Story" provides historical and cultural context.

The "Guildmaster's Observation" contains practical wisdom passed between experienced guildmasters.

Finally, the "Evolution of Implementation" reminds future developers how the dragon's gameplay systems may evolve alongside the C# curriculum.

## Species 001 — Stonejaw Drake

**Scientific Name** — Terradraco ferrumandibula

**Ecological Class** — Burrower

**Guild Profession(s)**

- Mining
- Construction
- Tunnel Surveying

**Habitat** — Mountain foothills, cave systems, abandoned mines, and rocky highlands.

Stonejaw Drakes prefer regions where vibrations travel easily through solid rock.

**Diet** — Primarily mineral-rich stone, cave fungi, crystal moss, and occasionally small cave creatures.

Despite their frightening jaws, they rarely hunt large animals.

**Temperament** — Patient.

Focused.

Quiet.

Persistent.

Stonejaws rarely become aggressive unless their nesting tunnels are threatened.

**Primary Instinct** — Stonejaw Drakes instinctively seek underground vibrations.

When they detect hollow spaces beneath them, they begin digging until they uncover the source.

This behavior evolved as a method of locating underground water, mineral deposits, and safe nesting caverns.

Humans eventually realized that these same instincts naturally revealed valuable ore veins.

**Guild Discovery** — Early miners noticed that abandoned Stonejaw tunnels often intersected rich mineral deposits.

Rather than competing with the dragons, guilds began allowing Stonejaws to excavate naturally before expanding the tunnels themselves.

Modern mining guilds still rely on this ancient partnership.

**Guild Applications**

- Excavating mines
- Locating ore veins
- Tunnel maintenance
- Underground construction
- Clearing cave-ins

**Strengths**

- Tremendous endurance.
- Exceptional digging ability.
- Excellent underground navigation.
- Can detect unstable rock through vibration.

**Weaknesses**

- Poor eyesight above ground.
- Slow movement in open terrain.
- Dislikes loud metallic noises.
- Becomes stressed when prevented from digging for extended periods.

**Care Difficulty** — Moderate.

Stonejaws require reinforced sleeping dens with compact soil or stone floors.

Without opportunities to dig, they become restless and destructive.

**Daily Routine** — Morning is usually spent expanding or maintaining tunnels.

Afternoons are dedicated to transporting excavated rock.

Evenings are often spent quietly arranging stones around their nesting area before resting.

**Interesting Behaviors** — Stonejaws collect unusually smooth stones and carefully arrange them outside their sleeping dens.

Researchers still debate whether these collections mark territory, attract mates, or simply satisfy a natural instinct.

No two arrangements are ever identical.

**Relationship with Humans** — Stonejaws bond slowly.

However, once trust is earned, they remain loyal for decades.

Experienced handlers often communicate using gentle tapping patterns rather than spoken commands.

**Relationship with Other Dragons** — They coexist peacefully with most Burrowers but generally avoid energetic aerial species.

Young Stonejaws frequently imitate older dragons while learning excavation techniques.

**Merchant Value** — Extremely High.

A single experienced Stonejaw can uncover mineral deposits worth many times its yearly maintenance cost.

**Species Story** — Among mining guilds, it is said that the first Stonejaw Drake was never captured.

Instead, an exhausted miner became trapped beneath a collapsed tunnel with no hope of escape. Days later, the rhythmic sound of digging echoed through the darkness. A Stonejaw, following faint underground vibrations, unknowingly broke through the rubble and opened a path back to the surface.

The dragon accepted neither praise nor reward. It simply continued digging deeper into the mountain, searching for another hollow chamber. Ever since, miners have believed that Stonejaws are not searching for treasure—they are searching for the heartbeat of the earth itself.

Yet despite centuries of partnership, no guild has discovered what ultimately determines where a Stonejaw chooses to dig next.

Guildmaster's Observation "Never rush a Stonejaw. They'll reach the ore eventually. Try forcing the pace, and you'll spend twice as long repairing collapsed tunnels."

## Species 002 — Hearthscale Drake

**Scientific Name** — Pyrodraco domesticus

**Ecological Class** — Highland Dragon

**Guild Profession(s)**

- Smithing
- Hospitality
- Cooking
- Construction

**Habitat** — Volcanic slopes, geothermal valleys, and rocky mountain settlements.

**Diet** — Charcoal roots, volcanic herbs, mineral ash, cooked meat, and heated stones rich in trace minerals.

**Temperament** — Gentle.

Patient.

Sociable.

Surprisingly affectionate.

Primary Instinct Hearthscales naturally regulate the temperature of their nests by slowly releasing body heat through specialized vent scales along their backs.

This instinct evolved to protect eggs from harsh mountain climates.

Merchant guilds later discovered that the same instinct could warm homes, kitchens, bakeries, bathhouses, and blacksmith forges.

**Guild Discovery** — Mountain villages began building communal hearths around naturally nesting Hearthscales.

Long before furnaces became widespread, entire settlements depended upon these dragons during winter.

**Guild Applications**

- Bakery heating
- Forge support
- Public bathhouses
- Kitchens
- Winter shelters

**Strengths**

- Reliable heat generation
- Calm disposition
- Excellent with children
- Low aggression

**Weaknesses**

- Performs poorly in tropical climates
- Requires frequent hydration
- Can become lethargic in extreme heat

**Care Difficulty** — Low.

Provided they remain hydrated and socially engaged, Hearthscales are among the easiest dragons to care for.

Daily Routine Most Hearthscales spend daylight hours helping kitchens and workshops before resting near communal gathering spaces during the evening.

**Interesting Behaviors** — They instinctively curl around cold stone walls while sleeping.

By morning, the surrounding room is noticeably warmer.

**Relationship with Humans** — Hearthscales actively enjoy companionship.

Many willingly seek out familiar workers after completing their daily tasks.

**Relationship with Other Dragons** — They are known to share resting spaces with smaller dragon species during cold weather.

**Merchant Value** — Very High.

One Hearthscale can replace several traditional furnaces while dramatically reducing fuel costs.

Species Story In many villages, children grow up believing every warm home has a Hearthscale somewhere beneath its roof.

Whether or not that is true, history records that countless mountain communities survived brutal winters only because these gentle dragons refused to abandon their nests, warming entire families with nothing more than instinct and patience.

To this day, retired Hearthscales are rarely sold. They are usually honored as lifelong members of the communities they once protected.

**Guildmaster's Observation** — "Treat them like equipment and they'll do their work. Treat them like family and they'll never want to leave."

**Evolution of Implementation**

- Temperature system
- Building warmth bonuses
- Worker morale
- Passive building effects
- Seasonal mechanics

**Future Expansion**

- Festivals
- Winter events
- Family bonding
- Village reputation
- Community interactions

## Species 003 — Riverveil Drake

**Scientific Name** — Aquadraco flumine

**Ecological Class** — River Dragon

**Guild Profession(s)**

- Water Management
- Fishing
- River Transport
- Exploration

**Habitat** — Freshwater rivers, lakes, floodplains, and wetlands.

**Diet** — Fish, river plants, freshwater shellfish, and aquatic mosses.

**Temperament** — Alert.

Curious.

Gentle.

Independent.

**Primary Instinct** — Riverveils constantly seek clean, slow-moving water.

They instinctively remove floating debris from drinking areas before settling down, maintaining healthy waterways without understanding why.

Guilds later realized this natural behavior greatly improved water quality around settlements.

Guild Discovery Early fishing villages noticed that streams inhabited by Riverveils consistently produced healthier fish populations and cleaner drinking water.

Rather than relocating the dragons, settlements grew around them.

**Guild Applications**

- Water purification
- River surveys
- Small cargo transport
- Fishery support
- Flood monitoring

**Strengths**

- Superb swimmers
- Excellent memory of waterways
- Calm under pressure
- Highly adaptable

**Weaknesses**

- Uncomfortable far from water
- Poor climbers
- Easily dehydrated in dry climates

**Care Difficulty** — Moderate.

Riverveils require constant access to clean water and regular opportunities to swim.

**Daily Routine** — River patrols begin shortly after sunrise.

Afternoons are spent assisting fishing crews or transporting cargo.

Evenings are devoted to quiet swimming before resting along sheltered riverbanks.

**Interesting Behaviors** — Before drinking, Riverveils carefully push floating leaves, branches, and debris toward the riverbank.

Even hatchlings perform this behavior instinctively.

**Relationship with Humans** — Riverveils respect patient handlers but dislike unnecessary noise.

Many eventually learn the daily routines of entire riverside communities.

**Relationship with Other Dragons** — They cooperate particularly well with Marsh Dragons and generally avoid Highland species.

**Merchant Value** — High.

Healthy waterways increase agriculture, transportation, fishing, and settlement growth simultaneously.

**Species Story** — Old sailors often joke that rivers remember every Riverveil that has ever lived.

No one knows why generations of Riverveils follow nearly identical migration routes despite never meeting their ancestors. Some scholars believe the rivers themselves shape their instincts. Others argue the dragons possess memories carried through bloodlines.

Whatever the truth may be, experienced captains trust a Riverveil's chosen path more than any map ever drawn.

Guildmaster's Observation "If your Riverveil refuses to cross a stretch of water, don't argue with it. Ask what it noticed that you didn't."

**Evolution of Implementation**

- Water quality values
- Fishing bonuses
- River pathfinding
- Seasonal flooding
- Async exploration missions

**Future Expansion**

- Weather systems
- Flood events
- Fish ecosystems
- River trade routes
- Water chemistry

End of Part I The species recorded in this volume represent only the beginning of the guild's understanding of dragonkind.

Many more await discovery, and even those described here continue to surprise the scholars and guildmasters who work alongside them.

As every seasoned dragon keeper eventually learns:

The day you believe you fully understand a dragon is the day it teaches you something new.
